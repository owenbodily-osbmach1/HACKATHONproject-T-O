Pulse

Privacy

Pulse counts problems in a room. Your votes and questions never say who you are.

What we never store

Pulse's data store (Elasticsearch), logs, dashboards and sponsor reports hold no names, no email addresses, no phone numbers, no IP addresses and no accounts. There is no password and no profile.

Joining a room (sign-in)

When the event turns sign-in on, your phone asks once for your first and last name, company or occupation, and email before it opens the room. Only the event team sees them, to follow up with you about the session: the AV crew can export the list with the staff key. They are kept in the server's memory only (gone when it restarts), never written to Elasticsearch, logs or telemetry, never shown to other attendees, and never linked to the random id that counts votes. A cookie remembers that this phone signed in, for 24 hours.

The one identifier

clientId is a random id generated on your phone. It is used only to count distinct devices — for example “3 different people cannot hear this talk” — and is never linked to a name, an email address or an account.

Booth reports

Booth reports never name a device. Any group with fewer than 3 devices is hidden, so a sponsor cannot pick out one visitor. Booth dwell is reported as an upper-bound range with the number of devices behind it.

The organizer overview

The public organizer page (/org) shows only room status: how many tickets are open, their type and how long they have been open. It never shows attendees' questions or the names of the crew.

Keys

The technician key and the speaker key open the staff pages. They are never written to logs or traces.
